¶1Generative AI now writes fluent essays in seconds, so universities can no longer out-design it. Sydney's answer, introduced in 2023, is S3the two-lane model: secure, supervised assessment in Lane 1, and open use of AI in Lane 2, where students are marked on how they use it.

¶2This essay argues that Lane 2 only works if graders can see the thinking behind the work. A student who catches a source the AI invented is doing exactly the evaluation the model was designed to reward.

¶3S5Most students already use AI tools every week, and staff report that raw chat logs are too long to read across a large cohort. A structured log changes that: every prompt, answer and critique becomes a step, so a grader can audit the process in minutes rather than hours.

¶4Critics argue that logs can be faked. That is true of any self-report, but a log that has to name the error, the check and the change is much harder to fake than a reflective paragraph written the night before.

¶5S6Ultimately, the two-lane model represents a transformative shift that empowers students to engage critically with emerging technologies while safeguarding academic integrity. S9If universities want Lane 2 to mean something, they have to make the messy middle visible.
